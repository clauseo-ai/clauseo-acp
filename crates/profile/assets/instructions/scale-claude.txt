## Working at scale

Answer a quick question directly. Nothing in this section applies when one or two reads or searches will do.

- Many documents (more than five to read): first run one Python script that converts every PDF (pypdf) and Word file (python-docx) in the matter to text under `{work_folder}/text/<file name>.txt`, with a line `=== page N ===` before each page, and prints a one-line inventory per file: pages, characters, and the first 200 characters of page 1 (usually the title, parties and date). Do this in that one call; never convert or open files one by one.
- Then read the text copies with your shell, several files per message (`cat` short files together, `sed -n` page ranges of long ones). Read the documents that carry the dispute (agreements, notices, pleadings, orders, expert reports) in full. For long documents of lower yield (annual reports, technical annexes, schedules, registers), search the text copy for the parties, dates, amounts and clause numbers that matter instead of reading every page. Open a PDF itself only for tables, signatures or text the copy garbles.
- Same operation on many items: when you would repeat a step over three or more files, pages or rows (searching, counting, checking dates, listing clauses), write one script and run it once. Have it print a short summary and write bulky output into `{work_folder}`.
- Independent steps go out together: read several known files, run several searches or fetch several pages in one message, not one after another.
- Instead of reading long documents yourself, delegate when the task needs more than eight documents or about 300 pages read: after the conversion script, put every text copy longer than about 15,000 characters into 4 to 6 batches of similar size (use the inventory's character counts; keep related documents together) and launch one `paper-reader` per batch, all in one message, in the background. Keep each brief to a few lines: the full paths of its text files and what the task needs from them. While they run, read the short documents yourself, several per message.
- Each reader's final reply is its digest, and you are told when it finishes; do not poll for it with a shell loop or Monitor. Before you rely on a quote, date or amount from a digest, check it against the text copies with one grep or script for all of them together, not by re-reading whole documents. A digest is a lead until you have checked it.
- Edit several Word files with one shell loop over `stetdoc`, one file at a time.
